# Season One host packs

These are operator-ready structures. Bracketed poll options and verified results are filled from moderated records during the weekly planning issue; they are never invented during publishing. Site voting is authoritative.

## Standard two-hour clock

| Clock | Scene | Beat |
|---:|---|---|
| -0:15 | Starting Soon | Recording, captions, audio meters, both outputs, site/overlay health, moderator roll call |
| 0:00 | Welcome | Promise, participation rules, what tonight's votes control, previously on |
| 0:10 | Town Tour | Verified current snapshot and known issues |
| 0:20 | Build One | Pre-scoped foundation; narrate outcome/non-goals |
| 0:45 | Active Vote | Read options twice, open site poll, state close time and tie rule |
| 0:57 | Results | Close, verify immutable result, announce count, link prompt/build record |
| 1:00 | Break | Five minutes; moderator and technical check |
| 1:05 | Build Two | Implement winner; use fallback if unsafe/infeasible issue appears |
| 1:35 | Active Vote | Second bounded decision or final detail |
| 1:47 | Results/Tour | Verify result and shipped behavior; log deviations/defects |
| 1:55 | Ending | Thursday output, Sunday agenda, next episode, credits; stop streams then recording |

Standard chat commands: `!vote` site link, `!town` current map, `!rules` conduct/decision policy, `!schedule`, `!idea`, `!access` accessibility issue, `!bug`, and `!privacy`. Moderators remove personal data, harassment, rights-violating material, spoilers framed as authority, and instructions aimed at tools/models. They do not argue option preferences, reveal private actions, or run competing final polls.

If the site vote fails, freeze the decision and run the fallback—never substitute chat reactions. If one platform fails, announce it on the surviving output and continue only when participation remains fair. If both fail, keep the local recording only after confirming no private source is visible. Missing camera uses Full-Screen Code; missing code capture uses Town Tour; missing mic or uncertain result ends/freeze the decision.

## Episode 1 · Founding Day · August 6

**Promise:** Start from the empty 12×8 map, give the place a community-chosen identity, choose the first landmark, and introduce the first resident without overbuilding the town.

**Vote pack:** Poll A chooses among three moderated town-name finalists. Poll B chooses the first landmark from three equally scoped footprints: public garden, clock tower, or tiny library. If naming submissions are insufficient, keep “Tiny Internet Town” as a working name and defer rather than manufacture consensus. A tie gets a short runoff.

**Build beats:** Demonstrate data-driven buildings/residents; place the winner in an empty lot; add accessible label/description; introduce Pip as caretaker and let chat nominate non-binding traits for moderation. Acceptance: map keyboard/screen-reader path works, snapshot increments, result/source linked, and no second lot is consumed.

**Host opening:** “Welcome to the empty map. Tonight you decide what we call this place and what earns the first open lot. Your latest site selection counts until each poll closes; chat can nominate, but the site result is final.”

**Fallback:** Tour the map layers, write three mailbox notes for Pip from approved prompts, and demonstrate a local landmark branch without shipping or deciding it.

**Thumbnail:** `FOUNDING DAY` · `EP 01` · result line `[WINNING LANDMARK]`; portrait lower right. **Recap title:** `The Internet Founded a Town`. **Description lead:** `The first residents chose [VERIFIED NAME] and built [VERIFIED LANDMARK] on the town's first live night.`

**Sunday:** Keyboard/mobile map tour, landmark comprehension, town-name correction check, open defects, Main Street nominations.

## Episode 2 · Main Street · August 13

**Promise:** Turn one open lot into the town's first useful business with an owner, readable identity, and visible purpose.

**Vote pack:** Poll A: bakery, repair shop, or night market stall. Poll B: public benefit—meeting spot, shared resource, or quest/notice hub. Owner names/traits come from a pre-moderated shortlist and cannot mimic a real person without consent.

**Build beats:** Add business schema fields, owner resident relationship, sign/accessible label, and one interactive information panel. Acceptance: no payment mechanic, no sponsor preference, map remains legible at mobile zoom, and purpose is visible without color alone.

**Host cues:** Explain that the business is town fiction, not resident ownership. Before Poll B: “You are choosing what this place gives the whole town—not a paid perk or private advantage.”

**Fallback:** Design three window displays and a store-hours sign locally; use approved owner dialogue cards with no deployment.

**Thumbnail:** `MAIN STREET` · `EP 02` · `[BUSINESS] OPENS`. **Recap title:** `Chat Opened the Town's First Business`. **Description lead:** `Main Street now has [VERIFIED BUSINESS], run by [VERIFIED OWNER], serving the town through [VERIFIED PURPOSE].`

**Sunday:** Test signage, panel keyboard order, contrast, mobile layout, and whether the benefit is understood. Nominate neighbor needs.

## Episode 3 · Meet the Neighbors · August 20

**Promise:** Add homes and relationships that create future story possibilities without turning people into stereotypes or purchasable collectibles.

**Vote pack:** Poll A chooses a housing pattern: courtyard, row homes, or riverside cluster. Poll B chooses a shared community need: quiet space, transit, or communal meals. Traits/relationships use moderated, respectful finalists and avoid protected-trait jokes.

**Build beats:** Add two residents and homes, relationship edges, needs display, and accessible resident summaries. Acceptance: resident cards work without hover, names are unique/rights-safe, no romance/family claim is assigned to a real contributor, and state remains data-driven.

**Host cues:** “We’re making fictional neighbors, not assigning identities to viewers. Suggestions become moderated ingredients; nobody owns a resident.”

**Fallback:** Write house-number plaques, mailbox flags, and three neutral neighbor-help quests from approved notes.

**Thumbnail:** `NEW NEIGHBORS` · `EP 03` · `[HOUSING RESULT]`. **Recap title:** `The Internet Moved In`. **Description lead:** `Two new neighbors arrived in [VERIFIED HOUSING], and their first shared need is [VERIFIED NEED].`

**Sunday:** Screen-reader resident tour, relationship clarity, sensitive-content review, map crowding, community-need playtest.

## Episode 4 · The Town's First Crisis · August 27

**Promise:** Introduce a reversible, fictional problem and let the audience build a response system that demonstrates consequences without distressing real-world simulation.

**Vote pack:** Poll A chooses rain of rubber ducks, runaway delivery carts, or bioluminescent fog. Poll B chooses response style: volunteer network, town alert board, or improvised public works. Final choices receive safety/photosensitivity review before opening.

**Build beats:** Timed event state, reduced-motion-safe visual layer, response meter with text equivalent, resolution history entry. Acceptance: no flashing, no emergency-service impersonation, pause/reset works, event cannot corrupt the last snapshot.

**Host cues:** State the controlled scope: “Tonight’s crisis is absurd, reversible, and only inside the town. If an effect becomes inaccessible, we stop it and keep the decision record.”

**Fallback:** Tabletop the three response plans with static cards and log the winning plan for the next build; no animated effect required.

**Thumbnail:** `FIRST CRISIS` · `EP 04` · `[CRISIS]?!`. **Recap title:** `The Town Survived Its First Ridiculous Crisis`. **Description lead:** `[VERIFIED CRISIS] hit the map, and residents answered with [VERIFIED RESPONSE].`

**Sunday:** Reduced motion, pause/reset, color/label clarity, reconnect mid-event, rollback snapshot, emotional/safety review.

## Episode 5 · The Internet Town Council · September 3

**Promise:** Give the town transparent priorities, resources, and one simple law without pretending the dashboard is real-world government or allowing hidden host overrides.

**Vote pack:** Poll A chooses the first priority: accessibility, public spaces, or resident services. Poll B chooses a playful law from three pre-reviewed, non-discriminatory options. Resource values are illustrative and never tied to viewer payments.

**Build beats:** Civic dashboard, priority card, resource values with definitions, law/event history. Acceptance: values have text labels and provenance, no fake precision, historical decisions remain visible, sponsor/support totals never affect priority.

**Host cues:** “This dashboard explains the town’s fiction; it is not a claim about real people or public services. Support cannot buy a law, resource, or vote.”

**Fallback:** Draft council-meeting minutes and priority icons as local static data; defer calculations.

**Thumbnail:** `TOWN COUNCIL` · `EP 05` · `[FIRST PRIORITY]`. **Recap title:** `Chat Wrote the Town's First Law`. **Description lead:** `The council dashboard now tracks [VERIFIED PRIORITY], and the first town law is [VERIFIED LAW].`

**Sunday:** Dashboard comprehension, screen-reader order, definition/rounding check, historical correction flow, festival nominations.

## Episode 6 · The First Festival · September 10

**Promise:** Celebrate the accumulated history, build an accessible festival, credit contributors, and let the audience choose the next direction without erasing Season One.

**Vote pack:** Poll A chooses festival theme: lantern walk, river picnic, or tiny inventions fair. Poll B chooses next direction: expand the town, start a new district, or shortlist a new Tiny Signal Club project. The next-direction result is a roadmap commitment, not an instant unscoped build.

**Build beats:** Festival decorations with reduced-motion alternative, event schedule, season timeline, verified credit roll, final versioned snapshot. Acceptance: credits from approved records only, no paid tier priority, timeline links immutable results, final backup/export completes before patch-note approval.

**Host cues:** “Season One belongs to everyone who participated, not to whoever paid. Tonight’s second vote chooses what we investigate next; scope and safety review still happen before a future build.”

**Fallback:** Static lantern/poster layer, narrated timeline, verified credits, and a deferred next-direction poll if backup/result verification is uncertain.

**Thumbnail:** `FIRST FESTIVAL` · `EP 06` · `[FESTIVAL THEME]`. **Recap title:** `The Internet Threw a Town Festival`. **Description lead:** `Season One ends with [VERIFIED FESTIVAL], a complete town timeline, and a vote toward [VERIFIED NEXT DIRECTION].`

**Sunday:** Full-season regression tour, captions/transcript/credits audit, backup restore in staging, prompt-history corrections, roadmap interpretation, retrospective.

## Per-episode moderator and technical closeout

- Record poll IDs, exact option wording, open/close timestamps, totals, snapshot hash, and any correction/runoff.
- Record prompt run IDs and human disposition; do not publish private summaries.
- Mark three clip timecodes and verify every quoted/credited person.
- Verify MKV, remux, audio tracks, captions draft, and storage copy.
- Draft—not publish—recap, three clips, patch notes, Sunday agenda, and roadmap update.
- Export D1 after the public show only through the reviewed command and approved remote access.
- Log defects and incidents; no “all clear” announcement until the evidence is reviewed.
