# Tiny Signal Club identity reset

Status: **rounds 01–02 rejected; round 03 is a working design system, not an approved public identity.**

## The problem with the rejected work

The earlier work used the name as an object-search prompt: signal became radio waves, receivers, plugs, CRTs, and patch cables; club became a mascot. Those things could decorate almost any retro-technology channel. They did not explain why this community exists, they did not connect cleanly to future projects, and their motion was ornamental rather than meaningful.

The midnight/cyan/coral treatment also defaulted to familiar “friendly technology” styling: dark UI, glowing accents, rounded cards, and nostalgic hardware. It made every asset feel like a variation of the same generic moodboard.

## Brand truth

Tiny Signal Club is a public creative workshop where small audience decisions combine into something none of the participants could have predicted alone.

- **Tiny** is the size of an input, not the ambition of the output.
- **Signal** is any useful contribution: a vote, idea, test, correction, constraint, or reaction.
- **Club** means recurring participation and shared history, not exclusivity.
- The transformation—many small inputs becoming one strange, verified result—is the memorable part.

## Single organizing idea: the open wildcard

The umbrella mark is an incomplete asterisk assembled from separate pieces. A small colored piece remains loose near the open arm.

The asterisk earns its place in four ways:

1. **Wildcard:** the audience can send the work somewhere unexpected.
2. **Footnote:** every shipped thing carries the story of who changed it and why.
3. **Work in progress:** the mark is visibly open, because the next contribution has not arrived yet.
4. **Assembly:** the pieces can reconfigure into project-specific symbols without losing the parent identity.

The mark must never become a generic sparkle. Its identifying construction is five heavy radial pieces, one deliberately absent arm, an irregular central void, and one detached square signal.

## Personality

Ingenious, mischievous, candid, participatory, and handmade with precision. It should feel closer to an excellent independent game publisher, experimental print studio, or community workshop than a streamer overlay package or software startup.

It is playful for adults. It does not use baby mascots, fake lore, forced jargon, or “cozy” as a substitute for an idea.

## Visual rules

### Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | Workshop paper | `#f1eadb` | Primary canvas; makes the identity physical and open |
| Primary | Soft black | `#171613` | Wordmark, wildcard pieces, body text |
| Action | Persimmon | `#ff5b3d` | The incoming contribution and decisive moments |
| Project | Ultramarine | `#3157ff` | Umbrella fields and Project One support |
| Surprise | Acid leaf | `#c8ef52` | Results, shipped states, occasional project accent |

Use paper and black most often. Accent colors carry meaning and should not all appear in every layout. Dark mode is a secondary inversion, not the brand’s default personality.

### Typography

- Display: an open-source, heavy grotesk with unusual width and strong lowercase forms; final candidate must be licensed and packaged locally.
- Text: a calm humanist sans.
- Utility: mono only for real data, timestamps, episode numbers, and verification—not decorative “computer” atmosphere.
- “TINY” may be set genuinely small. “SIGNAL CLUB” carries the visual weight. This turns the name’s potential weakness into hierarchy rather than making every word equal.

### Composition

- Editorial, asymmetric, and high-contrast.
- One strong focal action per frame.
- Large areas of workshop paper are intentional, not empty.
- Texture is limited to physical paper/ink evidence; never put noise over functional UI text.
- No rounded SaaS-card wallpaper, star fields, perspective grids, scanlines, neon glow, or piles of nostalgic electronics.

## Motion grammar

Motion always explains transformation:

1. One small persimmon square enters.
2. Existing black pieces make room or respond.
3. The pieces snap into a temporary complete form.
4. The form reconfigures into the current project/result.
5. One opening remains for what comes next.

Use slides, rotations, paper-like flips, and firm overshoot. A normal identity beat lasts 700–1400 ms. A full intro may stage several beats, but nothing drifts aimlessly in the background. Reduced motion shows the final meaningful arrangement immediately.

## Brand architecture

- **Tiny Signal Club:** open wildcard plus primary wordmark.
- **Tiny Signal Club presents:** small parent wildcard, then a project-owned title and palette.
- **Tiny Internet Town:** Project One may rearrange wildcard pieces into a map intersection or civic marker. Houses and residents never enter the umbrella logo.
- **The Modem Picnic:** may rearrange the pieces into a blanket/check pattern for the recurring event; modem hardware never enters the parent mark.
- Future projects inherit the pieces and motion grammar, not a forced town, broadcast, or mascot theme.

## Voice reset

Lead with plain action: “You choose a small part. We build and test what it changes.” Ritual language is optional seasoning.

Keep:

- “Small signals become big, strange things.”
- “You decided. We built it. Here’s what passed.”
- “Tiny Signal Club presents …”

Retire as default decoration:

- constant “transmission,” “frequency,” “receiver,” and “signal check” copy;
- claims that every viewer is a member before they participate;
- invented technical language viewers must learn.

## Approval tests

Round 03 cannot become the brand until the system passes all of these:

- The icon is recognizable at 24 px and in one color.
- The wordmark reads correctly in under two seconds.
- The mark cannot reasonably be mistaken for a radio, Wi-Fi logo, chat app, AI sparkle, or town brand.
- A Project One thumbnail and an unrelated future-project card visibly share a parent without looking identical.
- The motion still makes sense without audio and under reduced motion.
- A viewer can explain the idea as “small pieces becoming something together” without being coached.
- The system works on paper-white, black, and one-color applications.
