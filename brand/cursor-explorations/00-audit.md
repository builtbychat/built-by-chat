## 1. What the brand must communicate in under five seconds

A small contribution from someone else enters unfinished work and becomes part of a shared, tested result.

If a stranger only sees the mark and the words “Tiny Signal Club,” they should not think “telecom,” “chat app,” “AI product,” or “town game.” They should feel an open workshop that keeps changing because people keep showing up.

## 2. Why the rejected visual metaphors fail

| Asset family | Failure |
| --- | --- |
| Signal bubble + arcs (`brand/svg`) | Reads as chat + Wi-Fi. Any messaging or connectivity brand could wear it. The coral dot is a loading accent, not a participation rule. |
| Receiver / Patch Pal / Traveling Pulse (Round 02) | Hardware and mascot stories. Cute, ownable in a streamer sense, but they pin the club to retro electronics and character IP. Future non-game projects inherit baggage they do not need. |
| Midnight/cyan/coral system | Default “friendly tech night UI.” Atmosphere without a construction rule. |
| Open wildcard Round 03 (Codex) | Best idea so far: incomplete asterisk + detached contribution. Still provisional. Pill arms risk reading as sparkle, fan, flower, or Walmart-style burst if the missing arm is not obvious at 24 px. Geometry now matches the story; silhouette may still need work. |

Praise is not owed for effort. Round 03 earns a prototype seat. It has not earned production.

## 3. Name opportunities and risks

| Piece | Opportunity | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny | Hierarchy trick: set it literally small so the word becomes a design behavior. | Sounds childish if letterforms are soft, rounded, or mascot-adjacent. |
| Signal | Can mean contribution, vote, test result, annotation — not radio. | Immediately summons waveforms, antennas, Wi-Fi arcs. Forbidden as primary metaphor. |
| Club | Recurring belonging, membership-by-participation. | Exclusive country-club reading if typography feels gated or luxurious. |
| Full phrase | Distinct enough when hierarchy is sharp. | Easy to design as three equal words on a dark badge, which kills the idea. |

## 4. What a future-proof identity must do

Same construction rule must survive:

- channel avatar and favicon at 16–24 px, one color;
- livestream corner bug without becoming a Wi-Fi glyph;
- episode thumbnail next to unrelated project art;
- website masthead on paper-light and inverted dark;
- sponsor deck cover that does not look like a SaaS pitch;
- one-color print / sticker / rubber stamp;
- Tiny Internet Town cards that rearrange parent pieces without putting houses in the umbrella mark;
- a second project with no town, modem, or chat imagery at all.

If the mark needs a paragraph to stop looking generic, it fails.

## 5. Crowded territories to avoid

- Connectivity: Wi-Fi arcs, waves, towers, plugs, cables, CRTs.
- AI: sparkles, orbs, node graphs, purple/blue glow.
- Gaming: neon grids, XP badges, controller silhouettes, edgy monograms.
- Community SaaS: soft rounded speech bubbles, gradient orbs, “people icons.”
- Children’s brands: mascots with eyes, soft pastels, bounce-heavy lettering.
- Asterisk/sparkle logos used as “fun” decorations across retail and productivity apps — Round 03 sits near this fence.

## Ten measurable acceptance criteria

1. Recognizable silhouette at 24 px in one color.
2. Correct spelling “Tiny Signal Club” in the primary lockup with readable hierarchy in under 2 seconds.
3. No reasonable reading as radio, Wi-Fi, chat bubble, AI sparkle, or town map.
4. Viewer can state the mechanic as “small pieces become something together” without coaching.
5. Motion sequence shows contribution → assembly → open next slot; reduced motion shows final state only.
6. Tiny Internet Town and an unrelated project share parent DNA without identical layouts.
7. WCAG AA for functional text pairings on paper and soft black.
8. SVG marks contain no raster, no external font files required at runtime for the mark itself.
9. Play / Pause / Replay and keyboard controls work; no network dependency in motion studies.
10. Human decision fields exist; nothing is labeled approved without Nicholas’s explicit keep.

## Blunt bottom line

Codex’s open wildcard is the first concept with a real rule. Cursor’s job is not to decorate it. Cursor’s job is to pressure-test it with genuinely different routes and cleaner geometry studies, then put everything on the handoff page for a human call.
